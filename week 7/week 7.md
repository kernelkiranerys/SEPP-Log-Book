# Lecture 07: Scrum

---

## Agile vs Agility

- Simply following an "Agile process" does **not** guarantee true **Agility**.
- Agility is a **mindset** and a **trait** of individuals, teams, and organizations.
- True agility involves **adaptability**, not rigid processes.

---

## Origins of Scrum

- Based on 1986 research by **Takeuchi and Nonaka** on team-based product development.
- Emphasized:
  - **Autonomy**
  - **Cross-fertilization**
  - **Self-transcendence**

- Evolved into a software development framework by **Sutherland and Schwaber** in 1996.

---

## Scrum Principles

Scrum is grounded in Agile ideas and emphasizes:

- **Iterative Development**
- **Empirical Process Control**
- **Self-organisation**
- **Collaboration**
- **Value-based Prioritisation**
- **Time-boxing**

> Scrum is a **framework**, not a methodology.

---

## Scrum Roles

### Internal Roles

- **Product Owner (PO)**  
  - Represents the customer  
  - Manages the Product Backlog  
  - Defines and upholds business value

- **Scrum Master**  
  - Ensures the Scrum process is followed  
  - Removes impediments  
  - Acts as a servant-leader

- **Scrum Team**  
  - Cross-functional and self-organizing  
  - Generalist-specialists with overlapping skills  
  - Collaborative and co-located

### External Roles

- **Customer**
- **Other Stakeholders**

---

## Scrum Process

### 1. Initiation

- Begins with a **Stakeholder Meeting** and creation of a **Project Vision**.
- PO drafts a **Prioritised Product Backlog** based on **User Stories**.

### 2. Sprints

- Time-boxed periods of focused work (1–4 weeks).
- Begin with a **Sprint Planning Meeting**.
- Daily progress is tracked through **Daily Standups** (15 minutes, no discussion).
- End with:
  - **Sprint Review** (acceptance of work)
  - **Sprint Retrospective** (reflection and improvement)

### 3. Tracking

- **Scrum Board**: visual task tracking  
- **Burndown Chart**: visual progress tracking  
- **Velocity**: measures how much work a team completes per Sprint

---

## Key Scrum Concepts

- **Backlog Grooming**: PO refines and updates backlog during Sprints.
- **Incremental Delivery**: Each Sprint delivers shippable product features.
- **Constant Pace**: Avoid last-minute crunch by sustaining consistent work speed.

---

## Scrum-But

> “We use Scrum, but…”

- Most teams adapt Scrum to fit their context.
- Empirical process control encourages adaptation.
- This flexibility is natural and often necessary.
