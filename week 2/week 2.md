# Lecture 02: 

## Key Topics Covered

### 1. Socio-Technical Systems
A software system consists of three interconnected components:
- **Hardware** – The physical components.
- **Software** – Including:
  - System Software (OS, networking)
  - Application Software (end-user apps)
  - Framework Software (supporting platforms)
- **Wetware** – The users. Often the most important part.

> A successful system requires all three elements to function well together.

---

### 2. History of Software Engineering
- Emerged in the **1960s-1970s** due to growing system complexity.
- Early development was rigid and focused heavily on documentation.

---

### 3. The Waterfall Model
A linear process involving:
> Analyse → Design → Implement → Verify → Maintain

**Problems with Waterfall:**
- Long development cycles before delivering any value.
- Heavy reliance on documentation.
- Lack of user involvement leads to misaligned outcomes.
- Creates a “them and us” atmosphere between devs and users.

---

## Why Change Was Needed
- Faster tools and development cycles.
- More tech-savvy users.
- Rise of the internet and online services.

---

## Principles of Modern Software Development
- Measure success by **solving user problems**.
- If your work isn't used or doesn't help users, it's **waste**.
- Developers must engage with users and adapt to needs.

---

## ⚡ Agile Development

### Key Focus Areas:
- Iterative development.
- Rapid, sometimes continuous delivery.
- Developer-user collaboration.
- Flexibility and responsiveness.

### Agile Manifesto Values:
- **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools.
- **Working software** over comprehensive documentation.
- **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation.
- **Responding to change** over following a plan.

> Agile is a **mindset**, not a strict methodology.

---

## Agile Pitfalls

### Technical Debt
- Fast delivery may involve cutting corners.
- Needs intentional effort to "go back and fix."

### Feature Creep
- Excessive feature addition can reduce usability.
- **Jenkins’s Law of Software Products**:  
  > "Good software eventually becomes overloaded with unwanted features."
